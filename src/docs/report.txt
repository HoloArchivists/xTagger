XORVARIANCE / MVI AUTOCROPPER - FINDINGS
========================================
Data: 46 images in ./images (jpg/jpeg/webp/png/gif; RGB, RGBA, P, L and 1
modes). Measured with the Python prototype at 256 px tiles, lo=1, hi=9,
mode zero, minimum box 2x2. Every number in sections 1-4 was measured on this
set. The prototype only explores behaviour. The C kernel in section 5 defines
the metric, and nothing in it is shaped to match the prototype or any library.

Purpose: split crowded multi-subject frames into crops for wdtagger, then take
a weighted sum of tag overlap against the full-image pass. Dropping cloud and
sky tiles is wanted.


1. WHAT THE FOLD COMPUTES
-------------------------
Folding a tile with XOR gives the parity of each of its 8 bit-planes. The
popcount is the number of bit-planes with an odd count of set bits.

 a) Every flat tile with an even area folds to 0 (160 of 160 tiles here).

 b) A tile in which every value occurs an even number of times also folds to
    0, whatever it contains. In return.png a grain overlay repeats every
    128 px, so 54 of 144 tiles cancel to 0 even though their ranges go up to
    140. Wallpapers, dither patterns, transparency checkerboards and
    duplicated sprites cancel the same way. airbaloons.png (mode 1, almost
    entirely pixel-doubled) has 8 content tiles that fold to 0 for this
    reason.

 c) On all other tiles the popcount follows binomial(8, 1/2), measured on
    1883 non-flat tiles:
        popcount : 0     1     2     3     4     5     6     7     8
        measured : .052  .028  .117  .221  .262  .187  .096  .030  .008
        binomial : .004  .031  .109  .219  .273  .219  .109  .031  .004
    Most of the excess at 0 is case (b). Popcounts 2-8 carry no ranking: the
    only signal is whether the fold is 0 or not.

 d) Popcount 1 never occurs on a flat tile. Every hit at 1 is a non-flat tile,
    and they arrive at the binomial rate (2.8% measured, 3.1% expected).
       lo=0 : only flat tiles and even-repeat tiles.
       lo=1 : those, plus about 3% of all other tiles, picked without regard
              to content.
    The 309 violations at 256 px break down as 160 flat, 32 near-flat
    (range <= 16) and 117 textured.

 e) For crowded frames this matters because a lo=1 hit lands on a person as
    easily as on sky. In "Screenshot 2026-04-16 170916.png", tiles (1,3),
    (2,3) and (2,4) are the girl's head, arm and torso (ranges 234, 219, 220),
    and that is what split her. In hotcrap.png the hits are the cloud, the
    middle balloon and sky, all of them textured tiles. With lo=0, flat and
    repeating backgrounds still act as separators and the hits on subjects go
    away.

 f) Shifting the tile grid by 1 px kept 247 of 309 violations, including all
    160 flat ones. The hits that move are the textured ones.


2. INPUTS AND DETERMINISM
-------------------------
 - Parity flips on any change of 1 LSB. Flat and repeating tiles are stable
   across decoders. Textured tiles are tied to whichever decoder produces the
   bytes, so fix one decoder for the archive and the results are
   reproducible.
 - Fold the gray plane, and get it with the cheapest route for each source
   (see section 5). The choice of luma weights does not change what the
   metric detects: a flat tile is flat under any weighting, and so is a
   repeat.
 - XOR does not commute with weighted channel mixing. Fold after the gray
   reduction, or fold the raw interleaved bytes and skip gray altogether.
   The second option is a separate, equally valid metric: a flat tile still
   gives 0, and parity is taken over all channel bytes together.


3. EDGE AND ALPHA CASES
-----------------------
 - 430 of 2043 tiles were partial edge tiles. A flat tile with an odd area
   folds to its pixel value instead of 0 (2 cases in this set). Fix: if the
   area is odd, XOR the result with the first pixel. That is one scalar op
   per affected tile.
 - Narrow slivers (for example 256x5) go through the same test with very few
   pixels in them.
 - The prototype discards alpha. One RGBA image here had any alpha below
   255, and none had fully transparent pixels. In the archive, the RGB under
   alpha=0 is whatever the exporter left there. If transparency should count
   as background, fold the alpha bytes in, or force alpha=0 pixels to a
   constant with a vpcmpeqb/vpblendvb pair before folding.


4. SELECTOR FINDINGS
--------------------
 - 14 of 46 images have no violations at 256 px. Their only MVI is the whole
   image, which duplicates the full-image pass, so skip them before the
   selector and the tagger.
 - Mode dontcare with no box maximum always returns the whole image, so it
   needs --max-width and --max-height to be useful.
 - MVIs overlap. Tag weights based on area count shared tiles twice. Weight
   per tile instead: each tile casts one vote, split across the MVIs that
   cover it.
 - With the 2x2 minimum, isolated content tiles are left uncovered (5 on
   hotcrap.png). The full-image pass still sees them.
 - Aspect ratio is unconstrained (a 2x8 box is 4:1) and wdtagger's input is
   square. Add a maximum-aspect option if padded crops cause trouble.
 - Tile size sets how finely subjects separate. On the screenshot, 128 px
   with lo=1 gives 11 MVIs, against 4 at 256 px.


5. C99 / AVX2 KERNEL (COMET LAKE: AVX2, BMI2, POPCNT, NO AVX-512)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Everything stays 8-bit end to end: 32 pixels per ymm and one byte of result
per tile.

 Gray plane, by source:
 - JPEG and lossy WebP: they are already YCbCr/YUV. Take the Y plane straight
   from the decoder. That avoids colour conversion and chroma upsampling,
   and costs nothing.
 - Already-gray sources (L, palette resolved through a gray LUT, 1-bit
   expanded): fold the plane directly.
 - RGB/RGBA sources: gray = vpavgb(vpavgb(R, B), G), which is about
   (R + 2G + B) / 4 in two byte ops per 32 pixels, with no multiplies and no
   widening.
 - Deinterleaving RGBA, 32 pixels at a time (4 loads): vpshufb each load into
   per-lane [R0-3|G0-3|B0-3|A0-3], then vpunpck{l,h}dq and vpunpck{l,h}qdq
   give 32 R, 32 G and 32 B bytes. No lane-crossing vpermq is needed,
   because XOR does not care about pixel order. All channels end up with the
   same permutation, and the fold cannot tell. For RGB24, ask the decoder
   for RGBX output so it stays on this path, or fold the raw bytes (section 2)
   and skip deinterleaving entirely.
 - Raw-byte variant: one vpxor per 32 bytes. A 256 px RGB tile row is
   768 bytes, which is 24 aligned ymm loads and never straddles a tile.

 Fold:
 - One ymm accumulator per tile column for the current tile row. Up to
   16 columns stay in registers. Above that, the accumulators sit in a small
   aligned array.
 - Single pass: consume rows as the decoder emits them and vpxor each 32-byte
   chunk into its column's accumulator. Pixels are read once and never
   revisited, and prefetchnta keeps them out of the way. The tile bytes go to
   the GPU staging buffer without the fold ever touching them again.
 - Horizontal reduction of many tiles at once: when a tile row finishes, do
   not fold each accumulator to a scalar separately. Combine accumulators in
   pairs with vpunpck{l,h}* and vpxor, a butterfly that halves the bytes per
   tile at each level, until one ymm holds one byte per tile (32 tiles per
   register).
 - Popcount 32 tiles in parallel: a vpshufb nibble LUT on the low and high
   nibbles, then vpaddb.
 - Threshold: vpcmpgtb against the broadcast lo and hi values, then
   vpmovmskb. The violation mask for a whole tile row lands in one 32-bit GPR.
   With lo=0 this is simply vpcmpeqb against zero followed by vpmovmskb, and
   the popcount step disappears.

 Several tile sizes from one pass:
 - XOR is associative. Fold at the finest tile size (32 or 64 px), keep the
   per-tile bytes, and every coarser size is the XOR of its child tiles. That
   is a vpxor between adjacent rows of tile bytes, plus a byte-pair fold
   across columns. Changing tile size never touches pixels again.

 Selector on bitmasks:
 - Each tile row is a uint32 or uint64 mask. Runs of allowed tiles of width
   at least w are m & (m >> 1) & ... & (m >> (w - 1)), which takes log2(w)
   steps by doubling the shift. AND the row masks together for vertical
   extent. tzcnt/lzcnt find run edges and pext/pdep pull out runs.
 - Prime rectangles: grow a candidate downward by ANDing row masks, and stop
   when the run set shrinks. A rectangle is maximal when its row band is
   bounded on both sides and its run is bounded left and right.
 - Cover: rectangle cover sets are bitmasks over the grid, so set-cover
   branch-and-bound uses AND/ANDN/popcnt on a few machine words per set.
   The grid is tiny compared with the pixel data, and all of it fits in
   registers or L1.
